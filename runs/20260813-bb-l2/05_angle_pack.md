## Angle Component Definitions

- **Psychosocial Currency:** Emotional register the angle spends against.  
- **Core Idea:** One-sentence core friction / belief move.  
- **Headline:** ≤12 words, past, consequence or villain, no product.  
- **Hollow:** Specific named loss in a scene.  
- **Villain:** Relocatable concrete blame object.  
- **Dream:** Rendered sensory relief moment.  
- **Story Arc:** Setup → Action → Twist → Verdict.  
- **Product Fit:** Mechanism opens hollow → dream (door, not hero).

## Angles

### 1. The Night Breathing Safety Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Safety / Security  
**Core Idea:** Those night sounds aren't "just a phase" — they steal your ability to trust your own judgment.  
**Headline:** *I kept checking his breathing and still couldn't trust myself.*

**The Hollow:** You are alone in the dark doing second-guessing whether to call the nurse line. Every gurgle resets the scan. You cry, you check, you still don't know if you're overreacting or underreacting.

**The Villain:** A sealed guess dispenser — the bulb that gives you noise and hope without an answer — plus advice that shrugs night fear off as fussiness.

**The Dream:** You catch yourself by the bassinet and realise you aren't counting breaths. You're just standing there. Resting.

**Story Arc:** I came home careful and prepared. Then the nighttime congestion started and I became a conspiracy of one at 2 a.m. Saline. Steamy bathrooms. The hospital bulb. Nothing gave me proof. The twist wasn't that I was dramatic — newborns are nasal breathers, and I was fighting blind. Once I could see what came out and clear it with control, the math got quieter.

**Product Fit:** Baby Bubble's transparent chamber and regulated suction turn a blocked nose into something you can see and finish — so the night stops being a guessing game.

### 2. The Mould Betrayal Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Trust / Safety  
**Core Idea:** The tool you trusted to keep them safe may have been the thing you should have feared.  
**Headline:** *I sliced the hospital bulb and found black mould inside.*

**The Hollow:** You thought nightly clearing was care. The idea that you may have pushed hidden growth into a tiny nose sits in your stomach like guilt with a receipt.

**The Villain:** A sealed rubber bulb with no way to see, clean, or dry the inside — a design that traps moisture and hides black mould from the parent who trusts it.

**The Dream:** You watch them sleep and the replay stops. No flash of what was living in that rubber.

**Story Arc:** The hospital sent us home with the blue bulb and a nurse's ritual. Squeeze, insert, release. I obeyed for weeks. Then the smell. I opened it. The bloom inside ended the trust instantly. I didn't need another sealed chamber. I needed parts I could see and boil.

**Product Fit:** Baby Bubble comes fully apart with a transparent collection chamber — nothing sealed to grow, nothing to betray.

---

### 3. The Category Trap Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Agency / Control  
**Core Idea:** You've been buying the same broken idea in different packaging and calling it trying harder.  
**Headline:** *Three snot suckers later and nothing still came out.*

**The Hollow:** Frustration turns into self-blame. You line up tools that promised relief and still get empty results before the next feed.

**The Villain:** The category pattern itself — sealed and uncleanable, mouth-disgusting, or electric that fades and smells — sold as upgrades.

**The Dream:** One clear-out where thick mucus actually moves and you feel the night stop being product roulette.

**Story Arc:** I was solution-aware. Saline. Bulb. Mouth tube. A cheap electric. Each box felt like progress. At some point I saw the pattern: different prices, same failure modes. I stopped shopping claims and started shopping mechanism — can I see it, clean it, control it?

**Product Fit:** Baby Bubble is built around visibility, full cleanability, and regulated pull — the traits the failed category kept skipping.

### 4. The Unshareable Job Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Agency / Partnership  
**Core Idea:** When the tool requires someone's mouth, "help" isn't help — it's a job only one of you will take.  
**Headline:** *He offered to suction. I refused. I stayed alone.*

**The Hollow:** You become the permanent night technician. Not because he's useless — because the ritual is too intimate, too disgusting, too yours.

**The Villain:** Mouth-suction design that decides who can parent this task by demanding a mouth and a disgust tolerance.

**The Dream:** You hand him the device without a speech and without that knot in your stomach.

**Story Arc:** He offered. I said no. I hated how that sounded. The mouth tube made partnership feel impossible, so I stayed alone in the newborn trenches. The change wasn't a pep talk about teamwork. It was a tool that didn't need anyone's mouth.

**Product Fit:** Baby Bubble clears without mouth contact — hygienic enough to hand off, simple enough to share.

### 5. The Mean Mother Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Guilt / Regret  
**Core Idea:** Feeling cruel while helping doesn't mean you are cruel — it means the ritual was wrong.  
**Headline:** *I felt so mean clearing her nose I almost stopped.*

**The Hollow:** Every cry during clearing lands as evidence you're traumatizing them. Protection and self-hatred arrive in the same minute.

**The Villain:** Blind, irritating clear-out rituals (and the culture around them) that make necessary care feel like harm.

**The Dream:** A clear-out that feels like care — she settles, and you don't hate yourself for helping.

**Story Arc:** I wanted to protect her. Instead I felt mean. Wrestling a rolling baby, forcing a ritual that made both of us cry, I almost quit on the only thing that might help her breathe. The twist: the guilt was aimed at me when it belonged to a method that had no gentleness and no proof. A softer, visible clear let me help without auditioning as the villain.

**Product Fit:** Soft tip, controlled suction, and a chamber you can see — Baby Bubble makes help look and feel like help.

### 6. The No Answer Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Authority  
**Core Idea:** You can't be a capable parent in the dark if the tool never shows you what happened.  
**Headline:** *The bulb gave noise. It never gave me an answer.*

**The Hollow:** Competence collapses into guessing. You press, release, hear nothing useful, and still don't know if you did anything.

**The Villain:** Opaque tools with no visual proof — sealed guess dispensers that withhold feedback.

**The Dream:** You see the gunk in the chamber and know the job is done.

**Story Arc:** I kept trying to be the parent who fixes things. The bulb answered with noise. Not evidence. I didn't need more force. I needed sight. Once the clear-out was visible, I got my judgment back.

**Product Fit:** Baby Bubble's transparent collection chamber lets you see and hear the difference — answers instead of hope.

---

### 7. The Always ON Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Identity / Selfhood  
**Core Idea:** Congestion nights don't just steal sleep — they erase the person who can feel human.  
**Headline:** *Always ON through congestion nights erased who I was.*

**The Hollow:** World upside down. Sense of dread. Mentally ON until "mum" is the only identity left — and even that feels like failing.

**The Villain:** The opaque hospital bulb and the "just survive on less sleep" advice that treated hypervigilance as her new personality instead of a fixable blocked nose.

**The Dream:** You catch your own laugh in the kitchen glass — brief, ordinary — and recognise her again.

**Story Arc:** I used to be someone with edges and a nervous system that could power down. Then nighttime congestion made every hour a scan. People told me to lower expectations. The bulb gave me nothing to end the episode. The twist: I wasn't becoming a different person — I was stuck in an unfinished clear-out. When I could finally see the mucus leave and the night actually end, I felt more human without asking permission.

**Product Fit:** Baby Bubble's visible, regulated clear ends the episode that kept you always ON — the door back to yourself.

---

### 8. The Night Shift Couple Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Relational Equity  
**Core Idea:** When neither of you sleeps, you stop being partners and start being opposing shifts.  
**Headline:** *Neither of us slept until we fought the wrong enemy.*

**The Hollow:** Conversation shrinks to handoffs. Tenderness gets replaced by logistics. You're on the same team on paper and opponents by 3 a.m.

**The Villain:** Weak, opaque clear-out tools (bulb that barely works; mouth tube only one will touch) that keep both adults awake while you blame each other's shifts.

**The Dream:** Same kitchen light. Same two people. A quiet stretch where nobody is negotiating who is "up."

**Story Arc:** We were a team. Congestion made us a rota. We took turns resenting the wakeups while the real fight was a nose we couldn't reliably clear. The twist: we weren't failing at marriage — we were failing at a tool. Once the blockage could be finished without a disgusting handoff war, sleep came back in the same house — and we got on the same side again.

**Product Fit:** Baby Bubble clears the congestion running the night shift — visible, shareable, done — so rest and partnership can exist again.
