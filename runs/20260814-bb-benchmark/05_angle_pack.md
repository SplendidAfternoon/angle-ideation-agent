## Angle Component Definitions

- Psychosocial currency — emotional register the angle spends against.
- Core idea — one-sentence core friction / belief move.
- Headline — ≤12 words, past, consequence or villain, no product.
- Hollow — specific named loss in a scene.
- Villain — relocatable concrete blame object.
- Dream — rendered sensory relief moment.
- Story arc — Setup → Action → Twist → Verdict.
- Product fit — mechanism opens hollow → dream (door, not hero).

## Angles

### 1. The Night Breathing Unknown Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Safety / Security
**Core Idea:** Night sounds don't just wake you — they steal the ability to know what's true.
**Headline:** *I pressed my ear to his chest and learned nothing.*

**The Hollow:** You are the night watch. Ear to a tiny chest, counting rises that never become an answer. Every gurgle restarts the scan. You are not tired yet — you are uninformed, and that is worse.

**The Villain:** The hospital bulb that answers with a wet sound and hope. A sealed guess dispenser. It cannot show you whether the blockage left, so you keep checking his chest in the dark.

**The Dream:** You catch yourself by the crib and realise you aren't counting. You are just standing there. The room is ordinary.

**Story Arc:** I came home ready to be careful. Then the congestion started and I became a private check at 2am. Saline. Steam. The hospital bulb. Nothing proved he was getting air. The twist wasn't that I was dramatic — I was working blind with a tool that never showed the blockage. Once mucus could leave into a cup I could see, the chest-check loop had somewhere to end.

**Product Fit:** Baby Bubble's clear collection cup makes the mucus visible when it leaves, so night fear has an answer instead of another listen.

**Awareness stage:** problem_aware
**Test priority:** 1

### 2. The Unshareable Mouth Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Agency / Partnership
**Core Idea:** When the tool needs someone's mouth, help stops being shareable.
**Headline:** *Mouth suction made help a job only I would take.*

**The Hollow:** Help exists in the next room. He offered. Your body said no before your voice did. The night technician job became yours — not because he refused, because the ritual required a mouth.

**The Villain:** The mouth-suction tube (NoseFrida-class). It can move mucus and still decide who is allowed to parent the clear-out by demanding contact and a disgust tolerance.

**The Dream:** You pass him the device without a speech and without that knot. He does the clear-out. You stay a person in the doorway.

**Story Arc:** He offered. I said no. I hated how small that made us. Steam and the bulb had already failed; the mouth tube worked and still isolated me. The twist wasn't that I was controlling — the tool had chosen the parent. A clear-out that doesn't need anyone's mouth makes handoff a fact, not a pep talk.

**Product Fit:** Baby Bubble clears into a visible cup without mouth contact, so the job can move between you.

**Awareness stage:** solution_aware
**Test priority:** 2

### 3. The Sealed Chamber Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Trust / Safety
**Core Idea:** The tool you trusted to protect them may be the thing you should have feared.
**Headline:** *I couldn't wash the inside of the hospital bulb.*

**The Hollow:** Care had a receipt. You had been putting that rubber in a tiny nose. The thought of what lives in a chamber you cannot see, wash, or dry sits in the stomach like betrayal you performed yourself.

**The Villain:** A sealed hospital bulb — rubber you cannot open, inspect, or dry. Moisture stays. What grows stays. The parent who obeyed the nurse is the last to know.

**The Dream:** Open pieces on the counter. Nothing left to imagine growing in the dark. You watch them sleep and the replay stops.

**Story Arc:** A nurse handed us the bulb and a ritual. Squeeze, insert, release. I obeyed. Then the smell I couldn't unsee. I didn't need a stronger sealed chamber. I needed parts I could see and wash. The helper had been hiding.

**Product Fit:** Baby Bubble's clear cup comes apart to clean — no hidden chamber to betray the ritual you thought was care.

**Awareness stage:** solution_aware
**Test priority:** 3

### 4. The Blind Shopping Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Agency / Control
**Core Idea:** Trying harder with tools that show you nothing is not a plan.
**Headline:** *I kept buying blind tools that showed me nothing.*

**The Hollow:** Effort without feedback. You line up what you bought and still get "nothing comes out" before the next feed. Control didn't leave in a dramatic moment — it leaked out of empty results.

**The Villain:** Opaque weak clear-outs sold as upgrades: the sealed bulb, another tube, an electric that still doesn't show you the job. Same blindness, new box.

**The Dream:** The unused extras stay in the drawer. You don't add a fourth box to the line.

**Story Arc:** I was solution-aware. Bulb. Mouth tube. Something electric. Each box felt like trying. At some point I saw the pattern: I kept buying tools that could not prove a clear-out. The twist wasn't that I hadn't tried — I had been shopping force, not sight.

**Product Fit:** Baby Bubble's visible collection is the feedback loop the category kept skipping.

**Awareness stage:** solution_aware
**Test priority:** 4

### 5. The Night Rota Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Relational Equity
**Core Idea:** When neither of you sleeps, you lose the partnership before you notice.
**Headline:** *Nights turned us into a rota instead of a couple.*

**The Hollow:** You still live in the same house. Conversation shrank to who is on. Congestion that never finishes turning two people into logistics.

**The Villain:** The hospital bulb, then the mouth tube, failing in the same night so neither of you can clock out. Two tools. Still two people on shift.

**The Dream:** Same kitchen, shared quiet, both of you off-shift at once. Not a date. Just the house stopping.

**Story Arc:** We thought we were a team. Then congestion made us two tired shift workers. I blamed the roster. The twist was those two tools booking another watch every time they failed. A finished clear lets the house stop being a rota.

**Product Fit:** Baby Bubble's finished visible clear lets both of you clock out together instead of handing off another guess.

**Awareness stage:** problem_aware
**Test priority:** 5

### 6. The Cruel Clear-Out Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Guilt / Regret
**Core Idea:** Feeling like a monster while helping means the ritual is wrong — not that you are.
**Headline:** *Clearing her nose made me feel like I was hurting her.*

**The Hollow:** Protection and self-hatred arrive in the same minute. She screams. You finish anyway. "I feel so mean" becomes the only feedback the ritual gives you.

**The Villain:** The hospital bulb you squeeze through her scream — a clear-out that never shows it helped, so her cry becomes your only score.

**The Dream:** Her body goes heavy on your chest. You aren't narrating that you were cruel.

**Story Arc:** I wanted to protect her. Saline, then the bulb, then finishing through the scream. I aimed the guilt at my hands. The twist: a method with no sight will always feel like hurting. Seeing what left relocates the blame off the parent.

**Product Fit:** Baby Bubble lets you see the clear-out happen, so gentleness has evidence instead of a scream as the only score.

**Awareness stage:** problem_aware
**Test priority:** 6

### 7. The No-Answer Layer Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Authority
**Core Idea:** You cannot be a capable parent in the dark if the tool never shows what happened.
**Headline:** *Advice stacked up and none of it showed me the blockage.*

**The Hollow:** Competence collapses into guessing. You can follow every tip and still not know. Ambiguity is worse than a hard truth because there is nothing to be good at.

**The Villain:** The sealed guess dispenser plus stacked advice — sit up, steam, try the bulb — that never produces an answer layer. Noise. Never an answer.

**The Dream:** The last advice screenshot stays unopened on the lock screen. Your hands already have an answer.

**Story Arc:** I tried to be capable. Everyone had a sentence. The bulb gave noise. I stopped trusting my hands because nothing showed the blockage. The twist wasn't that I needed more slogans — I needed sight. Knowing is the answer the advice never was.

**Product Fit:** Baby Bubble's clear cup restores knowing: mucus either left or it didn't, and you can see which.

**Awareness stage:** problem_aware
**Test priority:** 7

---

### 8. The Erased Morning Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Identity / Selfhood
**Core Idea:** Endless ON nights make a temporary crisis feel like your new personality.
**Headline:** *2am clearing left no version of me by morning.*

**The Hollow:** Tonight's exhaustion projected onto forever. Only "mum" is left in the glass. You don't need a breakdown — you need a night that can end so a person can return.

**The Villain:** Just-cope lines — "this is just the newborn stage" — covering for a hospital bulb that never shows a finish, so erasure looks like personality.

**The Dream:** Catching your face in morning glass — ordinary recognition, not inventory. You still look tired. You also look like you.

**Story Arc:** I used to power down. Then 2am scans became the whole plot. I thought this was who I became. The twist wasn't a new identity — it was a bulb that never finished, wearing my face. A night that can end is a door back to self, not a personality transplant.

**Product Fit:** Baby Bubble's visible finished clear is a door back to a morning that isn't only the next watch.

**Awareness stage:** problem_aware
**Test priority:** 8
