## Angles

### 1. The Soft Lie Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Agency / Control  
**Core Idea:** Trying harder with pads that flatten is not control — it's a loop.  
**Headline:** *I kept buying gel pads that went flat.*

**The Hollow:** The drawer is full of dead cushions. Each one felt like diligence for a few days. Then the pancake returned and so did the hobble — effort with nothing to show.

**The Villain:** Compressible drugstore gel and foam pads that collapse under real load.

**The Dream:** Foot slides into the shoe and meets a firm arch answer — shape, not mush.

**Story Arc:** I bought the soft pad. Relief. Flat. Buy again. I called it a plan. The twist: softness was the trap. I needed structure under the arch, not another temporary cloud.

**Product Fit:** StrideForm's contoured arch and heel cushion redistribute pressure instead of compressing into another soft lie.

**Awareness stage:** solution_aware  
**Test priority:** 1

### 2. The Smaller Life Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Future Security  
**Core Idea:** Waiting doesn't pause what heel pain steals — it schedules a smaller life.  
**Headline:** *I scheduled a future without morning runs.*

**The Hollow:** The training calendar becomes a museum. You stop arguing with next month and start editing your life down to what a stabbed heel allows.

**The Villain:** Wait-and-stretch-only advice that never changes how the foot lands.

**The Dream:** Laces pulled tight without bargaining with a future version of yourself who quit.

**Story Arc:** I said it was temporary. Skipped runs stacked up. Stretching bought time that wasn't time. The twist: waiting was the whole plan — and it was shrinking me. I needed today's load to change.

**Product Fit:** A structured orthotic contour changes landing geometry now — a door back to mornings before the smaller life hardens.

**Awareness stage:** problem_aware  
**Test priority:** 2

### 3. The Flat Gel Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Trust / Safety  
**Core Idea:** The pad you trusted to protect the heel may be what quit on you.  
**Headline:** *The gel pad went flat and took my mornings.*

**The Hollow:** You believed the soft cushion was care. When it died midweek, mornings felt like betrayal with a receipt from the pharmacy aisle.

**The Villain:** Drugstore gel heel pads that flatten into a hard nothing.

**The Dream:** Open the shoe weeks later and still see contour — something that kept its shape.

**Story Arc:** The gel felt kind. I trusted it. Then it pancaked and the stab came home. The twist wasn't that I was dramatic — the helper failed. I stopped needing softer. I needed something that wouldn't quit.

**Product Fit:** StrideForm's non-collapsing arch contour is built to resist the pancake failure mode that burned trust.

**Awareness stage:** solution_aware  
**Test priority:** 3

### 4. The Bench Parent Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Identity / Selfhood  
**Core Idea:** Heel pain doesn't just stop runs — it rewrites who you think you are.  
**Headline:** *I became the bench parent on purpose.*

**The Hollow:** You choose the bench before anyone asks. Runner, chaser, play person — those labels feel like costumes from another season.

**The Villain:** The "just getting older" cope that turns hobbling into a personality.

**The Dream:** Mid-chase laughter without narrating the limp to yourself.

**Story Arc:** I used to play until the whistle. Then I sat out and called it maturity. The twist: it wasn't a new identity. It was a hostile landing wearing my face. I needed the foot supported, not a new story about who I'd become.

**Product Fit:** Pressure redistribution through a contoured insert opens the door back toward the person who could stay in the game.

**Awareness stage:** problem_aware  
**Test priority:** 4

### 5. The Snapped Morning Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Guilt / Regret  
**Core Idea:** Feeling cruel for sitting out means the morning ritual is wrong — not that you are.  
**Headline:** *I snapped when they asked me to play.*

**The Hollow:** The ask is innocent. Your heel has already voted. Sharp words arrive before you mean them; guilt stays after they run off without you.

**The Villain:** The first-step stab ritual that makes saying yes feel like self-harm.

**The Dream:** Tag in the yard — yes — without calculating the heel cost.

**Story Arc:** I wanted to be fun. Instead I barked and sat. I blamed my temper. The twist: the guilt was aimed at me when it belonged to a morning that started with a knife in the heel. Change the landing; the shame has less room.

**Product Fit:** Kinder first-step geometry from structured support reduces the spill of heel rage into parenting.

**Awareness stage:** problem_aware  
**Test priority:** 5

### 6. The Stretch Fog Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Authority  
**Core Idea:** You can't be a capable problem-solver if every tip ignores how you land.  
**Headline:** *Everyone had a stretch; nobody fixed my landing.*

**The Hollow:** Threads, videos, "just rest." Competence turns into guessing. Ambiguity feels worse than a hard, clear answer under the foot.

**The Villain:** Just-stretch / just-rest advice lines with no underfoot structure.

**The Dream:** Standing up knowing what you're standing on — a visible, physical answer in the shoe.

**Story Arc:** I followed the tips. Still stabbed. More tips. The twist: I didn't need another slogan. I needed a load answer. Once the arch had a contour job, judgment came back.

**Product Fit:** StrideForm states a clear mechanism — redistribute plantar pressure — so authority returns with a why.

**Awareness stage:** solution_aware  
**Test priority:** 6

### 7. The Hallway Limp Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Social Status / Competence  
**Core Idea:** The limp isn't just pain — it's a competence costume you didn't choose.  
**Headline:** *I timed hallway walks so nobody saw me limp.*

**The Hollow:** You map routes and pause behind doors. Public floors become stages. The hobble feels like a verdict on how put-together you are.

**The Villain:** Flat fashion sneakers treated as "fine for work" with zero support.

**The Dream:** Crossing the open-plan floor without timing your steps between desks.

**Story Arc:** I joked about being stiff. I timed the hallway. I parked far to hide the walk. The twist: my shoe had no structure — the audience wasn't the enemy. Support in the shoes I already wear was the missing piece.

**Product Fit:** StrideForm inserts into daily shoes so public walking stops being a performance of pain.

**Awareness stage:** problem_aware  
**Test priority:** 7

### 8. The Unequal Walk Angle

**Psychosocial Currency:** Relational Equity  
**Core Idea:** When one heel owns the morning, the partnership becomes a rota.  
**Headline:** *My heel turned us into unequal walking partners.*

**The Hollow:** Dog walks, school runs, errands slide to them. You say thank you and mean sorry. Tenderness thins into logistics.

**The Villain:** Unfinished heel mornings that dump movement chores onto one partner.

**The Dream:** Same sidewalk. Shared pace. No apology speech for the limp.

**Story Arc:** We used to walk together. Then I sat out and they carried it. Quiet scorekeeping started. The twist: it wasn't love failing — it was a load imbalance. Fix the morning landing and the rota can loosen.

**Product Fit:** Stable underfoot mornings make shared walks possible again — StrideForm as the door, not the romance fix.

**Awareness stage:** problem_aware  
**Test priority:** 8
